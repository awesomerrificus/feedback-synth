Help

How the loop works & shortcuts

Two instruments are wired into each other. The Sound tab is a synth that plays a picture: a scan line sweeps across it in tempo and turns bright spots, colour, detail and motion into notes, chords and drums. The Visuals tab is a video synth that listens: it cuts between sources on the beat and drives its effects from the sound.

Here the synth's picture is the visuals output, and the visuals hear the synth. A kick can trigger a zoom and a cut, the cut floods the frame with new light and motion, and that becomes the next notes and hits. Feedback and echo effects make it ring for longer; Synth looks at pulls it back towards a steady picture of your own.

Start with a linked scene, then change one side at a time. Low Response (Sound tab) makes the synth react fast; high Response smooths it out. If the loop runs away, lower the visual effect amounts or move Synth looks at to the left.

Help

How it works & shortcuts

In this app the synth's "picture" is normally the visuals output itself (see the Loop tab), so what you hear changes what you see, which changes what you hear. You can also load an image or video, or turn on the webcam, and mix it in. A scan line sweeps across the picture in time with the tempo, like a playhead over a graphic score.

Image score: each step of the scan, the brightest spots in that slice of the picture become notes. Higher in the frame is higher in pitch, and everything is kept in your key and scale. Pad holds chords that change each bar, picked from the picture's colour (or where the light sits). Spectral scanner plays the whole scan line at once as a spectrum, the way early optical synths read drawn sound. Drums come from texture along the scan line (bottom of the frame → kick, middle → snare, top → hats) and from movement: wave at the webcam to play hits. Texture is filtered noise that follows the detail in the picture.

Each mapping picks one picture feature: brightness, darkness, contrast, colour (saturation), hue, red / green / blue, detail (edges), motion, where the light is (light X / Y), where the movement is (motion X / Y), the brightness under the scan line, or a slow LFO. Base is the resting value, amount is how far the picture pushes it (left of centre pushes the other way).

Sampler (each has Volume, Mute and Unload, which empties it; use ＋ Add sampler for up to three, each with its own audio, mode and mappings; how many are open is saved with scenes, the audio itself is not): load any audio (a loop, a beat, a field recording), or tap ● Record to capture a take (on a computer it records the microphone in the page, tap Stop when done, silence at the ends is trimmed; on a phone it opens the camera app, which records sound reliably, and the sound of that clip is sliced), and it is cut at its hits, like a beat-slicing sampler. Slice sensitivity and Max slices set how many pieces you get; tap the waveform to hear a slice. Then the picture plays them: Motion pads splits the frame into a grid and movement in a square fires its slice (great with the camera); Scan line plays the slice picked by the brightest spot as the line sweeps; Movement hits fires on sudden movement, left-to-right picks the slice; In order plays the slices back on the beat, and movement makes it stutter. Snap to the beat keeps it in time. The audio file is not saved with scenes: load it again next time. MIDI: slices go out on channel 11 from note 36.

Scenes store every sound setting. They're kept in this browser; Export saves them to a file you can back up or Import elsewhere.

Shortcuts are listed in the Loop tab.

Help

How it works & shortcuts

Load a track, add a pool of images and video clips, press play. The synth listens to the music in real time and cuts between your sources on the beat, layering a second source on top and running effects driven by the sound.

Each effect in section 4 picks one sound feature: level (overall loudness), bass / low-mid / mid / high (frequency bands), brightness (spectral centroid: dark vs bright timbre), beat (kick-driven pulse), onset (any new hit or note), flux (how fast the spectrum is changing), slow/fast LFOs and a random value that changes on each beat. Base is the resting value, amount is how much the sound pushes it.

Recording captures exactly what you see plus the track's audio, and downloads an MP4 (or WebM on browsers without MP4 recording) when you stop or the track ends. It records in real time, so leave the tab visible while it runs.

Shortcuts are listed in the Loop tab.

Offline render analyses the track frame by frame and steps every video clip to its exact frame, so the result is smooth at any resolution even if your computer can't play it live. The same seed always gives the same edit. In Chrome it asks where to save and writes straight to disk, so long 4K renders don't fill up memory.

Live: pick your audio interface under Input, then Live input. Use the tap clock if the room is too noisy for beat detection. Open the output window on the projector. MIDI learn: press it, click any dashed control, move a knob or hit a pad.

Effect modules run after the main look. Video feedback feeds each frame back into itself zoomed, rotated and shifted, for tunnels and spirals. Video delay/echo keeps the last 120 frames and layers up to 4 delayed copies (taps) on top, timed in ms or synced to the beat (tap tempo or detected BPM). Echo feedback sends the echoes back into the delay so they repeat and fade. Amount, mix and feedback can follow the music like any other effect, and every module setting is saved in scenes.

Lens, analog and motion effects run last, on the finished picture: pixel drift (the picture as dots, squares, an LED grid or halftone; each cell keeps its colour while it wanders and scatters with the sound; shape is set in its panel), dot lens (a drifting grid of little ball lenses; more = bigger, inverted discs), fisheye, handheld shake, dirty lens, film look, bloom, lens fringing, depth blur, VHS, CRT, datamosh smear (strongest just after a cut), mirror tiles, slit-scan, stutter/strobe and neon edges.

Sampler (video + sound): load a video or a sound file, or tap ● Record (on a phone this opens the camera app, which records picture and sound; on a computer it records your webcam and microphone in the page; up to 12 s of video, 8 s on phones, or 60 s of sound). The clip is cut into slices at its sound hits (or at picture changes, or into even pieces; Slice sensitivity and Max slices give more or fewer), shown as a filmstrip and waveform you can tap. Plays picks Video + sound, Video only or Sound only. The music plays it: a new slice on each beat (random or in order), on every hit, picked by the bass, or scrubbed by loudness. Speed (backwards to the left), Loudness speeds it up, Reverse chance, Stutter, Slice length and Ping-pong shape both picture and sound. Volume sets the sound, Picture level fades the picture, Mute silences and hides it, Unload empties it. Up to three samplers; their settings are saved with scenes, the clips are not.

Generative sources (section 2) are algorithms that draw live, driven by the music, and join your pool like any clip: Liquid light (a 60s oil-projector light show: coloured oils merge and split in a wobbling dish, swirl with the mids, swell with the bass and get shoved on kicks; pick a colour set or your own three oils and light colour, with or without the projector circle, under the source list), Moiré orbs (glowing fine-line spheres, ribbed orbs, wavy blobs, bubbles, a horizon glow or a spiral tunnel: bass swells them, mids ripple the edges, highs make the lines shimmer; pick shape and palette under the source list, or leave both on Auto to change with the music), Ink bloom (ink-in-water strands that burst on kicks, turn with the mids and drift with loud passages; pick its ink and paper colours under the source list), Woven ribbons, Sliced weave and Mirrored chevrons (op-art weaves whose rows slide on the beat), Disorder grid (after Vera Molnár / Georg Nees), Random walk (after Frieder Nake), Oscillons (your track as an oscilloscope figure, after Ben Laposky), Harmonic dots (after John Whitney), Cube studies (after Manfred Mohr), Chance collage and Cut-up strips (built from your own images and clips, after Arp, Höch and Burroughs), Rotoreliefs (after Duchamp), Rule drawing (after Sol LeWitt), Squares & stripes (after Albers, Stella and Agnes Martin) and Event score (your own words, after Fluxus). They're remembered between sessions.

Scenes store every setting in sections 3 and 4 (mappings, cuts, blend, crossfade, beat sensitivity). They're kept in this browser; use Export to save them to a file you can back up, share or Import on another computer. Each scene gets a button under Live performance that you can map to a MIDI pad.
